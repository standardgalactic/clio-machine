\chapter{Optimisation Inside Admissible Space}
\label{ch:chapter-06-optimization-admissible-space}

\section{Chapter Preface}
Modern machine learning often foregrounds optimization and assumes feasibility is implicit. CLIO reverses this ordering. Preference over invalid reconstructions is not merely suboptimal; it is semantically meaningless.

The chapter therefore distinguishes admissibility from objective quality. Feasible reconstruction defines the domain of meaningful choice, and optimization refines only within that domain.

The resulting mathematics clarifies exactly when Lagrangian and KKT machinery can be trusted to produce admissible and globally valid optima.

\section{Derivations and Formal Results}
Define feasible set
\[
A=\{x:g_i(x)\le0,\ h_j(x)=0\}.
\]
Optimization is
\[
x^\star\in\arg\min_{x\in A}J(x).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Unconstrained optimum may be inadmissible]
\[
\arg\min_{x\in\stateSpace}J(x)
\]
need not intersect \(A\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Counterexample: \(\stateSpace=\mathbb R\), \(J(x)=x^2\), \(A=[1,\infty)\). Unconstrained minimizer is \(0\notin A\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Lagrangian and KKT system]
\[
\mathcal L(x,\lambda,\mu)=J(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i g_i(x)+\sum_j\mu_j h_j(x).
\]
KKT conditions include stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient optimality under convexity]
If \(J\) and all \(g_i\) are convex, each \(h_j\) affine, and Slater's condition holds, then any KKT point is globally optimal.
\end{theorem}
